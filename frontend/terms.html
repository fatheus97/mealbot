8. Allergens, diets and safety

Mealbot is a helper, not a guarantee, and not medical or dietary advice. Always check the actual product labels yourself. For a diagnosed condition or allergy, talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian.

When you declare allergens, we do two things: we instruct the AI to avoid them, and then we run a separate, automatic check over the generated ingredients. If that check cannot produce a plan that avoids your allergens, we refuse to show you one rather than showing you something doubtful.

Our allergen term lists are written in English. When a recipe is generated in another language, the AI must also supply an English name for each ingredient, and we check both that name and the one in your language. An ingredient it gives us no English name for cannot be checked at all, so we treat the whole plan as failed and generate a new one rather than showing it to you.

What that check cannot do — please read this properly:

  • On a non-English recipe it leans on the AI's own English naming. The English name described above is the AI's own work. If it names an ingredient incorrectly in English, our check reads that incorrect word, finds nothing, and lets the recipe through. The check is only as good as that translation.
  • Sulphites are not covered by the automatic check. Whether sulphites must be declared depends on how much remains in the finished product, which cannot be worked out from a recipe. We instruct the AI to avoid them, but there is no deterministic backstop.
  • Cooking steps are checked in English only. We check both the ingredient list and the recipe steps, and we instruct the AI never to use anything in a step that isn't in the ingredient list. But steps are written in your language, and that check reads English — so on a non-English recipe, something introduced only in the method ("brush with butter") relies on that instruction rather than on our check.
  • Edits you make are checked, but not blocked. If you change a recipe and the result contains an allergen you declared for that plan, we tell you — and we still save it. It is your recipe, so the decision stays yours. This check reads English ingredient names, so if you type an ingredient in another language we will not recognise it.
  • It cannot know about cross-contamination, "may contain" warnings, or reformulated products. Only the label in your hand can tell you that.
  • If you declare no allergens, nothing is screened at all. Your declaration is stored in your browser, so clearing site data or switching device can reset it — check it is still set before you rely on it.

Halal and kosher options bias the recipes toward compliance. They are not certification and involve no verification of sourcing or preparation.

Diets associated with medical conditions — gluten-free, low-FODMAP, diabetic, keto and similar — are offered as recipe styles. Low-FODMAP in particular is normally a temporary, dietitian-supervised elimination process, not a permanent way of eating. Mealbot does not supervise it and is not a clinical tool.

Baby food. The infant option applies general weaning guidance — no honey, no added salt or sugar, no whole nuts, no raw egg, no unpasteurised dairy, no high-mercury fish and no obvious choking hazards — and, like allergens, it now has an automatic check behind it: we scan the ingredients and steps and refuse to show a meal that fails.

That check reads ingredient names, so there is a lot it cannot see. It cannot tell whether a food was cut to a safe size, cannot judge whether cow's milk is being used in cooking or given as a drink, does not check total daily salt or sugar, and does not assess whether a meal is nutritionally adequate. It is also stricter than UK guidance on one point: it treats all raw and lightly-cooked egg as off-limits, because it cannot see whether an egg is British Lion marked.

Infant feeding carries real risks including choking and allergy introduction. Use this as inspiration only, and follow your paediatrician or health visitor.
